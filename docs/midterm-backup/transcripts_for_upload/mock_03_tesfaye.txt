Counselor: Good evening, Mr. Bekele. Is it okay if I record this?
Student: Yes. Please, call me Tesfaye. It is fine.
Counselor: Thank you. You can skip anything, and please don't mention immigration documents or numbers.
Student: I understand.
Counselor: How long have you been here, and what's a normal week like?
Student: Six months now. We arrived in May. I work at the distribution center, the night shift, from ten in the evening to six-thirty in the morning, five nights. In the day I sleep until noon, then the children come home from school at three, and I help them with homework. My wife works part-time at a hotel, housekeeping. On Sunday we go to church, the Ethiopian Orthodox church in St. Paul.
Counselor: What has been the hardest part so far?
Student: Honestly, we are blessed. Many people wait their whole life for this chance. I cannot complain.
Counselor: I hear that. Even when things are going well, there can be hard parts.
Student: Yes. Of course. The winter, we have not seen it yet. Everyone tells us to be afraid.
Counselor: What kind of work did you do in Ethiopia?
Student: I taught mathematics. Grade nine to twelve, in Adama. Fifteen years. Some of my students are engineers now. One is a doctor.
Counselor: Have you looked into teaching here?
Student: Yes. I went to the education department office. They said my degree must be evaluated by an agency, it costs money, and then I need a Minnesota license, and the tests, and maybe more courses. The lady was kind. She gave me a paper with eleven steps. I have the paper.
Counselor: Where are you with the eleven steps?
Student: Step one. Step one is to send the transcripts from my university. To get documents from there now, it is not simple. And the agency costs two hundred and something dollars. Right now the children need winter clothes. So the paper waits.
Counselor: How is the night shift on your body?
Student: It is work. A man works. The sleep is not good, I will say. In the day the house is noisy, the neighbors, the children. Sometimes I sleep four hours. Then at work I am... slow. My supervisor is younger than my students. He said I must increase my rate.
Counselor: How did that feel?
Student: It is his job to say it.
Counselor: How do you get to work at night?
Student: The bus going is okay. Coming back, the first bus is at 6:50, so I wait forty minutes. In the winter, they tell me this will be a problem. A coworker sometimes gives me a ride, but not every day.
Counselor: Is there anything you've had to figure out here that works differently?
Student: The health system. My daughter had ear pain. We went to the emergency room because I did not know where else to go. We waited six hours. Then a bill came. I am still not sure if the insurance paid it or we must pay. The letter says "This is not a bill." Then why does it have a number in dollars?
Counselor: That confuses a lot of people.
Student: My wife says, call them. I say, I will call. I have not called.
Counselor: Do you feel pressure to make this move worth it for anyone?
Student: Everyone. My mother, my brothers. They are proud. Every month I send something home. My mother has diabetes, her medicine is expensive. When they call, they say, "How is America?" and I say, "It is good, it is good." What else will I say?
Counselor: What would you say, if you could say anything?
Student: I would say... in Adama I was Teacher Tesfaye. People greeted me in the market. Here, I am a number on a scanner. But this is for my children. My children will be the teachers here. So. It is fine.
Counselor: Thank you for sharing that.
Student: It is nothing.
Counselor: What has helped you get through the harder days?
Student: The church. And my children. My son is in fourth grade, he already says he wants to be an engineer. And mathematics, I still do it. I tutor two children from church, for free.
Counselor: Has anything made you hesitant to ask for help?
Student: I don't like to ask. When you ask, you become... a person who asks. In our community, people know everything. If I go to the food shelf, someone will see. We are fine.
Counselor: Is there anything we didn't ask about that matters to you?
Student: If there is a faster way to get the teaching license, I would like to know.
Counselor: How comfortable were you being recorded? Anything you held back?
Student: It was fine. I am a teacher, I am used to speaking. Maybe I spoke more than I planned.
